Five brands. Five ways in. How global brands entered India in 2026, mapped to my Global Marketing course at XLRI.
Every entry mode trades control for risk. At the bottom of the ladder you spend little and you risk little, but you don't control much of what the customer sees. At the top, you own all of it.
Licensing. Off-White and Brand Concepts. First store in Bengaluru, April 2026, with five flagships planned in three years. You test the market cheaply, but someone else runs your shop floor.
Franchise. Lululemon and Tata CLiQ. First store at DLF Promenade, Delhi, plus Tata CLiQ online. A proven concept, run by a partner. The course flags the catch: picking and training that partner.
Exclusive partner. SKIMS and Reliance Brands. Announced August 2026, with flagships in Delhi and Mumbai. Reliance runs the stores and the online channels. You're borrowing the partner's local muscle, and that's the whole point.
Full ownership. UNIQLO opens in Jaipur on 13 November, its 22nd store in India. It owns the subsidiary outright. You control everything. You pay for everything.
Own it, online first. IKEA plans to invest over $2.2 billion, growing from 6 stores to 30. In new cities it sells online before it builds a store.
When the partner walks away. Jubilant won't renew Dunkin' after 31 December. 27 outlets left. In a franchise, your partner can end your market. It's that simple.
The entry mode is a brand decision. If the product is the brand, a partner can sell it. If the store is the brand, run it yourself.
Licensing, Off-White. Franchise, Lululemon. Partner-operated, SKIMS. Owned, UNIQLO. Owned and online-first, IKEA. Partner exit, Dunkin'.
