Dunkin' planned 500 stores in India. It's leaving with 27.

Jubilant FoodWorks won't renew the franchise when it ends on 31 December, about fifteen years after the deal was signed.

The easy explanation is that India drinks tea.

But Dunkin' never really arrived here as a coffee-and-donut brand. The first store opened in Connaught Place in 2012 as an all-day eatery. By 2013, burgers were the hero of the menu. Later the burgers were pulled back, and the focus went back to donuts and coffee.

In Global Marketing at XLRI, we studied the four choices a brand has when it goes abroad: sell the product as it is, modify it, design something new for that market, or build a flexible global product.

Dunkin' tried most of them, one after another.

One line from our licensing slides fits even better. A brand runs into trouble when its name goes on a product too far removed from its original success.

So my read is this. Adaptation isn't about how much you change. It's about whether customers can still tell who you are once you have.

A Dunkin' burger answered a question nobody here was asking.

Which global brand do you think adapted to India without losing itself?
